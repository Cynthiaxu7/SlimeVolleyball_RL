\section{Introduction}

Deep RL exposes methodology questions that supervised pipelines do not:
non-stationary data, sparse credit assignment, and tight coupling
between exploration and the data the model trains on. The same
optimizer and loss family produce radically different outcomes once a
policy is allowed to choose its own training data.

We study these effects on \textbf{Slime
Volleyball}~\citep{slimevolleygym}, a two-player physics game with a
12-dim observation, $\textsc{Discrete}(6)$ actions, and episodes of up
to 3000 frames. The environment is small enough that a full ablation
matrix fits in a weekend's GPU budget and large enough that subtle
algorithmic choices produce statistically meaningful differences. We
benchmark every variant via (a) deterministic-greedy reward against the
scripted baseline (\texttt{eval\_R}) and (b) a 1500-match round-robin
Elo ladder over 19 trained variants, and we deploy each policy as an
ONNX model in a browser playable for end-to-end reproducibility.

\textbf{Contributions.} (1) A $2{\times}2{\times}2$ ablation over
Dueling, Double, and $n$-step on a shared trunk that exposes a
destructive interaction: $n$-step \emph{without} Double DQN is worse
than vanilla DQN. (2) A self-play scaffold combining a FIFO opponent
pool~\citep{silver2016alphago,vinyals2019alphastar} with baseline
mixing, plus the empirical finding that pure-pool self-play
\emph{underperforms} mixing the scripted baseline. (3) A PPO
entropy-collapse case study with a four-change rescue ($+3.6$ reward
swing). (4) An ``ecosystem effect'': a $\times 10$ obs-scale bug in the
in-pool baseline produced a lineage of trained agents (Elo $\sim$1800)
that beat a correctly-trained agent at $\sim$8:1 in head-to-head play.
