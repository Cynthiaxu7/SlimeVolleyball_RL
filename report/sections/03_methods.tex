\section{Methods}
\label{sec:methods}

\subsection{Architectures}

\paragraph{Dueling MLP (DQN family).}
All DQN variants share a 12{-}128{-}128 ReLU trunk and a Dueling
head~\citep{wang2016dueling}. The trunk's 128-dim feature is fed into a
value head $V_\phi : \mathbb{R}^{128} \to \mathbb{R}^{64} \to \mathbb{R}$
and an advantage head
$A_\phi : \mathbb{R}^{128} \to \mathbb{R}^{64} \to \mathbb{R}^6$.
Following the standard mean-subtraction reparameterization,
\begin{equation}
Q_\theta(s, a) \;=\; V_\phi(s) \;+\; \Big( A_\phi(s, a) - \tfrac{1}{|\mathcal{A}|}\sum_{a'} A_\phi(s, a') \Big),
\label{eq:dueling}
\end{equation}
which keeps $V$ and $A$ identifiable up to constant shift. The total
parameter count is $\approx 28\text{k}$, intentionally small so that overfitting
to opponent-specific quirks is harder. The plain DQN ablation cell
(\texttt{--no-dueling}) replaces the dual head with a single
$128 \to 64 \to 6$ MLP at the same parameter scale.

\paragraph{Actor--critic MLP (PPO).}
PPO uses a Tanh trunk
$\mathbb{R}^{12} \to \mathbb{R}^{128} \to \mathbb{R}^{128}$ with
orthogonal initialization
(std $= \sqrt{2}$), then splits into an actor head producing logits
$\ell \in \mathbb{R}^6$ (final layer initialized with std $= 0.01$ so
the initial policy is near-uniform) and a critic head producing a
scalar value $V_\psi(s) \in \mathbb{R}$ (final layer std $= 1.0$). The
ONNX-facing forward returns logits only --- since
$\arg\max_a \mathrm{softmax}(\ell)_a = \arg\max_a \ell_a$, the JS
frontend's \texttt{argmax} contract works without a softmax layer.

\subsection{Losses and Optimization}

\paragraph{DQN loss.} For a sampled minibatch, with $n$-step return
$R_t^{(n)} = \sum_{k=0}^{n-1} \gamma^k r_{t+k}$, the target uses the
Double DQN~\citep{vanhasselt2016double} decoupling
\begin{equation}
y_t = R_t^{(n)} + (1 - d_t)\, \gamma^{n_{\text{actual}}}\,
       Q_{\theta^-}\bigl(s_{t+n},\; \arg\max_{a'} Q_\theta(s_{t+n}, a')\bigr),
\label{eq:double-target}
\end{equation}
and the Huber (smooth $L_1$) loss
$\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{DQN}}(\theta) = \mathbb{E}_{(s,a,y)}[\,\mathrm{Huber}(Q_\theta(s,a) - y)\,]$.
The \texttt{--no-double} ablation switch replaces $\arg\max$ over
online with $\max$ over the target, recovering vanilla DQN's
positively-biased target. Prioritized Experience
Replay~\citep{schaul2016per} is a drop-in change to the buffer and
adds an importance-sampling weight $w_i \propto (1/p_i)^\beta$ to the
per-sample loss, with $\beta$ annealed $0.4 \to 1.0$.

\paragraph{Distributional C51 head.}
The C51~\citep{bellemare2017c51} variants replace the per-action scalar
$Q$ with a categorical distribution over 51 atoms in $[v_{\min}, v_{\max}]$,
optimized with cross-entropy against the projected Bellman target. Expected
$Q$ is recovered as $\sum_i p_i z_i$ inside \texttt{forward} so the ONNX
contract stays $(B, 6)$.

\paragraph{PPO loss.} Standard clipped
surrogate~\citep{schulman2017ppo} with
GAE-$\lambda$~\citep{schulman2016gae} advantages:
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{PPO}} = -\,\mathbb{E}_t\!\Big[ \min\bigl( r_t(\theta)\,\hat A_t,\; \mathrm{clip}(r_t(\theta), 1{-}\epsilon, 1{+}\epsilon)\,\hat A_t \bigr) \Big]
+ c_v \mathcal{L}_V - c_e \mathcal{H}[\pi_\theta],
\label{eq:ppo}
\end{equation}
where $r_t(\theta) = \pi_\theta(a_t|s_t) / \pi_{\theta_{\text{old}}}(a_t|s_t)$,
$\mathcal{L}_V$ is a PPO2-style \emph{clipped} value MSE, and
$\mathcal{H}[\pi_\theta]$ is the per-state action entropy.

\paragraph{Optimizer and regularization.}
All variants use Adam (lr in Table~\ref{tab:hp}). DQN regularization is
(a) a soft Polyak target update
$\theta^- \leftarrow (1-\tau)\theta^- + \tau\theta$ with
$\tau = 5\!\times\!10^{-3}$ every gradient step, and (b)
gradient-norm clipping at 10. PPO regularization stacks
(a) entropy bonus $c_e$, (b) per-minibatch advantage normalization,
(c) gradient-norm clip at $0.5$, and (d) target-KL early-stop: if the
running mean of $\widehat{\mathrm{KL}}_t = (r_t-1) - \log r_t$ within a
PPO epoch exceeds $\delta_{\mathrm{KL}} = 0.02$, remaining update epochs
are skipped --- this turned out to be the single most important add to
prevent entropy collapse on this task.

\subsection{Beyond-class-material methodology}

The course rubric requires highlighting at least one technique beyond
standard syllabus material. We highlight two.

\paragraph{(A) Self-play with a FIFO opponent pool and baseline mixing.}
Vanilla DQN/PPO recipes assume a fixed environment with a stationary
reward function. Slime Volleyball is competitive, so we instead train
against a maintained pool of past selves --- the same scaffolding used at
much larger scale by AlphaGo~\citep{silver2016alphago} and
AlphaStar~\citep{vinyals2019alphastar}. Concretely, every
$100\text{k}$ environment transitions, the current online network is
\emph{snapshotted} (frozen state-dict copy) and pushed into a FIFO pool
capped at $30$ snapshots. At every episode boundary, each parallel env
draws an opponent independently: with probability
$p_{\text{base}}$ from the scripted baseline,
otherwise uniformly from the pool. The two-player API
\texttt{env.step(a, otherAction=a\_opp)} lets us collect 16-way
parallel rollouts where each worker can be playing a different
opponent. Crucially, we found that $p_{\text{base}} = 0$ (pure pool)
catastrophically loses generalization to the scripted baseline, even
when the pool is seeded with the strongest available checkpoint
(Section~\ref{sec:results}). This is methodologically important:
``pool quality $>$ baseline quality'' is a tempting intuition that does
not survive contact with the data.

\paragraph{(B) PPO entropy-collapse rescue.}
Our first long PPO run terminated at \texttt{base\_R}~$=-3.4$ (near
sweep-loss) despite \texttt{pool\_R}~$\approx +1$. Diagnosis from
TensorBoard logs: the policy entropy
$\mathcal{H}[\pi]$ collapsed from $\approx \log 6 \approx 1.79$ to
$0.108$ by step 1.4M (a 16$\times$ drop), at which point the policy was
deterministic enough to win against snapshots of itself but wholly
unable to handle the stylistically different scripted baseline. Our
rescue intervention combined four changes: $c_e: 0.001 \to 0.01$
(10$\times$ entropy bonus), rollout length
$128 \to 256$ (better
GAE estimates over the 3000-frame episodes), linear lr anneal $\to 0$
across the run, and target-KL early stop with $\delta_{\mathrm{KL}}{=}0.02$.
The rescue run finished at \texttt{base\_R}~$= +0.2$ with
$\mathcal{H} = 1.42$ --- a $+3.6$ reward swing and a near-restoration
of policy stochasticity. We present this as a worked example of
production-style RL debugging: read the diagnostic scalars, identify
the failure mode (here, premature commitment), and intervene with a
small, justified config cluster rather than a hyperparameter sweep.
