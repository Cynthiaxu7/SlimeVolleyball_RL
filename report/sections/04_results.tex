\section{Results}
\label{sec:results}

We evaluate on two complementary axes. \texttt{eval\_R} is the deterministic
greedy reward per episode against the scripted baseline (5 episodes per
checkpoint, $\in [-5, +5]$). Ladder \emph{Elo} is computed from a
1500-match round-robin among 19 trained variants ($\times 2$ clones per
variant for variance averaging, $K=32$, init $1500$); the scripted RNN
baseline is included as a reference player.

\subsection{Component ablation: $2\times2\times2$ over Dueling, Double, $n$-step}

Table~\ref{tab:ablation} summarizes the five-cell DQN ablation; bars in
Figure~\ref{fig:ablation-bars} visualize the corresponding \texttt{eval\_R}.
All cells share the trunk, target network, replay buffer, and training
budget (5M transitions); the only differences are the indicated algorithmic
switches.

\caption{\texttt{eval\_R} per ablation cell. The two cells without Double

Five findings stand out.

\textbf{(F1) Dueling alone barely helps.} Cell A $\to$ B is only $+0.2$
\texttt{eval\_R}. On a 12-dim observation the value/advantage decomposition
of \citet{wang2016dueling} provides marginal gain --- there are few states
where ``the action does not matter,'' which is exactly the structural
signal Dueling exploits.

\textbf{(F2) Double DQN is the single biggest win.} Cell B $\to$ C
is $+0.6$ \texttt{eval\_R}, the largest delta from a single component
in the matrix. With a small / noisy Q-net, $\max_a Q_{\theta^-}(s', a)$ is
a positively biased estimator of the optimal next-state value, and the
Double decoupling
$Q_{\theta^-}(s', \arg\max_{a'} Q_\theta(s', a'))$ \citep{vanhasselt2016double}
removes most of that bias.

\textbf{(F3) $n$-step \emph{without} Double is actively harmful.} Cell B
$\to$ D is $-1.8$ \texttt{eval\_R}. Each additional step in the $n$-step
sum injects further off-policy bootstrap noise; with only $\max$
selecting and evaluating the lookup, the over-estimation bias compounds
across the horizon. Re-introducing Double on top of $n$-step (cell D
$\to$ E: $+1.8$) almost fully restores the deficit. This is a destructive
\emph{interaction}, not a sum of marginal effects --- a fact obscured in
the original Rainbow paper~\citep{hessel2018rainbow} which only ablates
\emph{removing} components from the full stack.

\textbf{(F4) $n$-step + Double $\approx$ $n=1$ + Double on this task}
(cells C and E within noise). The practical takeaway: if only one upgrade
over plain DQN is affordable, add Double; $n$-step is a multiplier on
Double, not a standalone improvement.

\subsection{Self-play opponent mixing}

Table~\ref{tab:selfplay} summarizes the three self-play runs at 20M
timesteps. Setting $p_{\text{base}} = 0$ (\texttt{purepool}) collapsed
generalization to the baseline: \texttt{base\_R} dropped to $-1.2 \pm 0.7$
and ladder Elo fell to $1454$ ---~225 below $p_{\text{base}}{=}0.4$ ---
despite seeding the pool with the strongest available checkpoint.

proxy: pool diversity and pool quality are not the same axis.

\subsection{PPO entropy collapse and rescue}

Figure~\ref{fig:ppo-entropy} contrasts the policy entropy
$\mathcal{H}[\pi_\theta]$ trajectory of the failed PPO run
(\texttt{ppo\_lowent\_20m\_fixed}) with the rescue run. The failed run
collapses from $\mathcal{H} \approx \log 6 \approx 1.79$ to $0.108$ by step
$1.4\text{M}$ --- a 16$\times$ drop --- after which the policy is too
deterministic to escape its local optimum. The rescue intervention (10$\times$
entropy bonus, 2$\times$ rollout, linear LR anneal, target-KL early stop)
keeps the entropy at $\mathcal{H} = 1.42$ throughout and lifts
\texttt{base\_R} from $-3.4 \pm 1.5$ to $+0.2 \pm 0.4$, a $+3.6$ swing.

\subsection{Ladder Elo and \texttt{eval\_R} disagreement}

Figure~\ref{fig:elo-ladder} shows the ladder ranking for all 19 variants.
The headline observations:

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Self-play vs.\ cold (same algorithm) is the largest single
gain in the project.} \texttt{v1\_selfplay} (Elo $1795$) vs.\ \texttt{v1}
cold (Elo $1282$) is a $513$-Elo gap, $\sim$94\% expected-win.
\item \textbf{The scripted RNN baseline ranks 6th of 19.} Only the top
five trained variants reliably beat it.
\item \textbf{Full Rainbow with C51 + NoisyNet collapsed.} Both
\texttt{rainbow\_full} (Elo $1084$) and \texttt{rainbow\_eps} ($1071$) lose
$95\%+$ of ladder matches. Diagnosis: the C51 support
$[v_{\min}, v_{\max}] = [-5, +5]$ matches episode-return extremes
exactly, so probability mass concentrates on the boundary atoms and
expected-$Q$ values become nearly degenerate. A retrain with $[-10, +10]$
support is the predicted fix; the simpler ``Rainbow-Lite''
(Dueling + Double + $n$-step + PER) reaches Elo $1690$ on the same
budget.
\item \textbf{\texttt{eval\_R} can disagree with ladder Elo.}
\texttt{plain} DQN (Elo $1520$) outranks \texttt{dueling} ($1453$)
despite a worse \texttt{eval\_R} ($-0.6$ vs.\ $-0.4$), and \texttt{ppo\_fixed}
(failed; Elo $1540$) outranks \texttt{ppo\_rescue} (Elo $1497$) despite a
$+3.6$ \texttt{base\_R} advantage. \texttt{eval\_R} measures
\emph{performance against one specific opponent} (the scripted baseline);
Elo measures \emph{average performance against the entire ecosystem}.
A policy that hyper-specializes on the baseline can lose to other
trained agents that play differently.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Negative results are scientifically valuable.} Of five
hypotheses we expected to confirm --- ``Rainbow always helps,''
``$n$-step is a free win,'' ``self-play $>$ cold,'' ``pool quality $>$
baseline quality,'' and ``\texttt{eval\_R} predicts Elo'' --- only one
(\emph{self-play $>$ cold}) survived contact with the data. The
resulting redesigns (Double DQN as a prerequisite for $n$-step,
baseline mixing as a prerequisite for self-play, ladder evaluation as
a complement to single-opponent reward) are the most useful
methodological output of this study.
